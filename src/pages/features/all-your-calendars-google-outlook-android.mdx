# All your calendars on Android

The family calendar lives in iCloud, work lives in Google, and your Nextcloud hums away in a closet next to the router. The school publishes its terms on a website, and the birthdays are buried in your contacts. KashCal shows all of it in one app.

## How calendars get in

There's no single way to reach every calendar. Apple speaks CalDAV, Microsoft and Google prefer their own APIs, and schools and sports leagues hand out a web address. So KashCal takes calendars four ways, plus its own:

Every calendar shows up in the same views. To hide one, uncheck it in the navigation drawer.

## iCloud and other CalDAV servers

iCloud is a CalDAV server with a few rules of its own, so it has its own setup:

- Apple doesn't let other apps use your Apple ID password. Make an app-specific password at [account.apple.com](https://account.apple.com).
- You don't type a server address. KashCal finds your calendars after you sign in.
- Family Sharing calendars keep their own permission rules.

For Nextcloud, Fastmail, Radicale and the rest, open **Settings**, tap **Calendar accounts**, then **Add CalDAV**, and enter the server address, username and password. The providers people have tested are listed in the [README](https://github.com/KashCal/KashCal#tested-caldav-providers).

## Google and Outlook come in through your phone

Microsoft doesn't offer CalDAV, and Google points apps to its own API. Building a separate connection for each would mean OAuth, token refresh and each vendor's policies. Android already keeps those accounts in sync, so KashCal reads them from there. These are device calendars.

- Google: calendar sync is on for your Google account in Android's account settings.
- Outlook or Microsoft 365: install Outlook for Android and turn on **Sync calendars**.

Then, in KashCal:

1. Open **Settings**, then tap **Device calendars**.
2. Turn on **Show device calendars** and allow calendar access.
3. Turn on each calendar you want to see.

People pair KashCal with Google Calendar, Outlook for Android, Microsoft 365 and Samsung Calendar this way. Others subscribe to the `.ics` address Outlook or Google publishes, as a one-way feed.

You can add, edit and delete events on device calendars. The account's own app syncs the change, and a calendar the account marks read-only stays read-only. KashCal rings their reminders; to silence them, turn off the **Device calendars** heading in **Settings** > **Calendar notifications**. Colors come through when the account sets them: change a Google calendar's color in Google Calendar and KashCal picks it up within minutes. Some accounts, such as Outlook, set none. To make one event stand out, give it one of the [92 named colors](/features/beautiful-calendar-android).

## Calendar feeds

A calendar feed is one-way. You paste its address, and KashCal refreshes it once a day, or as often as you set. Holidays, school terms, sports seasons and conference schedules are the usual ones. Most Outlook calendars and any public Google Calendar publish one too.

- Public holidays: open **Calendar feeds (ICS)** in Settings, tap **Add holiday calendar**, and pick your country. No address needed.
- Feeds you already have: if you subscribed to feeds in another app, they may be saved on your iCloud or CalDAV account. Tap **Add from account** to see them and turn on the ones you want.
- A `webcal://` link opened with KashCal offers to subscribe. An `.ics` file or attachment opens as an import, and other apps' **Add to calendar** requests land in KashCal.

Repeating events with one-off changes, such as a cancelled or moved occurrence, come through, including the feeds Microsoft Exchange produces.

## Offline calendars

Some plans don't belong on a server: gift ideas, a health log, a journal. KashCal's offline calendars stay on your phone, never sync, and other apps can't read them. Make as many as you like in **Settings** > **Offline calendars**, and back them up to a folder you choose. More in [the offline calendars post](/blog/offline-calendars-android).

## Birthdays from your contacts

Birthdays and anniversaries live in your contacts, not on a calendar. KashCal reads them from Android's contacts and shows them as yearly all-day events. Open **Settings**, tap **Birthdays & anniversaries**, turn on **Contact Birthdays** or **Contact Anniversaries**, and allow contacts access. Each gets its own calendar in the drawer.

## How it stays private

There's no KashCal server. iCloud and CalDAV traffic goes from your phone to your server, over HTTPS unless you type an `http://` address. Feeds, holiday calendars included, are fetched straight from your phone. Device calendars are synced by Android and the account's own app, not by KashCal. The passwords you enter are encrypted with the Android Keystore (AES-256-GCM) and left out of device backups. Details, including what Android backup saves, are on the [privacy page](/docs/privacy/overview).

## Where to start

- iPhone family, Android phone: connect [iCloud](/docs/sync/providers/icloud).
- Nextcloud, Fastmail or another CalDAV provider: **Settings** > **Calendar accounts** > **Add CalDAV**.
- Google Calendar or Google Workspace: sign in to Google in Android with calendar sync on, then turn on its calendars under **Device calendars**.
- Outlook or Microsoft 365: install Outlook for Android, turn on **Sync calendars**, then turn on its calendars under **Device calendars**. Or add its `.ics` address as a feed.
- Holidays, school, sports: **Calendar feeds (ICS)** > **Add holiday calendar**, or paste the feed's address.
- Just you: make an [offline calendar](/docs/sync/offline-calendars). No account needed.
- Birthdays and anniversaries: **Settings** > **Birthdays & anniversaries**.

## Common questions

### Can KashCal show my Google Calendar on Android?

Yes. Add your Google account in Android's system settings and let it sync. Then open KashCal **Settings**, tap **Device calendars**, turn on **Show device calendars**, allow calendar access and turn on the calendars you want. Your Google events show up alongside everything else, in the colors set in Google Calendar.

### Does KashCal sync with Outlook?

Through your phone. Microsoft doesn't offer CalDAV. Install Outlook for Android and turn on **Sync calendars** in its settings, then turn on those calendars under **Device calendars** in KashCal Settings. You can also subscribe to an Outlook `.ics` share link as a calendar feed.

### What is the difference between CalDAV, calendar feeds, and device calendars?

CalDAV is two-way sync over an open standard. A calendar feed is a one-way subscription to a calendar's web address that refreshes on a schedule. Device calendars are accounts already set up on your Android phone, like Google or Outlook. Android keeps them in sync, and KashCal shows the ones you turn on.

### Will calendars from different sources show up together?

Yes. iCloud, CalDAV, feed, device, offline and birthday events appear in the same views. Uncheck any calendar in the navigation drawer to hide it.

### Where do birthdays and anniversaries come from?

From your phone's contacts. Open **Settings**, tap **Birthdays & anniversaries**, turn on **Contact Birthdays** or **Contact Anniversaries** and allow contacts access. KashCal turns each date into a yearly all-day event on its own calendar.
